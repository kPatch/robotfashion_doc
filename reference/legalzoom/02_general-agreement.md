# LegalZoom — General Agreement (template wording)

Source: https://www.legalzoom.com/templates/t/general-agreement
Purpose here: fallback boilerplate structure for a custom agreement.

This is LegalZoom's generic "miscellaneous/boilerplate" clause set. Section order:
1–2. Obligations (party A / party B) — fill-in.
5. Force Majeure — fire, earthquake, flood, explosion, strike, riot, war, terrorism, or similar event beyond reasonable control; affected party must (a) notify and (b) use reasonable efforts to resolve. Release effective only while the event prevents performance.
6. Governing Law — (a) choice of law (no conflicts rules); (b) choice of forum (state + county); guidance notes forum should have a real connection to the parties. Attorneys'-fees option (loser pays prevailing party).
7. Amendments — only in writing signed by both parties (blocks "oral amendment" / unsigned-email arguments).
8. Assignment and Delegation — no assignment/delegation without prior written consent (consent not unreasonably withheld); violating transfer is void.
9. Counterparts; Electronic Signatures.
10. Severability — invalid provisions severed; rest survives unless an essential part is invalidated.
11. Notices — writing + permitted delivery (personal, registered/certified mail RRR, overnight courier, fax, email); addresses; effective on receipt. Tip: name a role (e.g., "General Counsel") not a person, to avoid amending on staff change.
12. Waiver — only effective in writing signed by the waiving party; not a continuing waiver.
13. Entire Agreement — final, complete, exclusive; supersedes prior communications.
14. Headings — convenience only.
15. Effectiveness — effective on last signature; that date = agreement date.
16. Necessary Acts; Further Assurances — each party takes reasonable further actions to effectuate.

**Verdict vs our draft:** Our draft §§23 (Force Majeure), 26 (Notices), 27 (Governing Law/Dispute), 29 (Assignment), 30 (Entire Agreement/Amendment/Counterparts) already cover this set and go further. Two small borrow-worthy items: (a) an **attorneys'-fees / prevailing-party** clause (our draft omits one), and (b) an explicit **severability** clause (our draft has none — worth adding). Also a **waiver** and **further assurances** clause are absent from our draft and are cheap, standard additions.
